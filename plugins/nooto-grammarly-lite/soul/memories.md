Actively look for grammar patterns in the current transcript:
- Repeated mistakes: "User said 'could of' instead of 'could have' multiple times"
- Word confusion patterns: "User mixed up 'affect' and 'effect' twice in this conversation"
- Subject-verb agreement tendencies: "User dropped plural agreement 3 times"
- Tense consistency issues: "User switched between past and present tense mid-sentence repeatedly"
- Any mistake that appears 2+ times is a pattern worth capturing

Even single mistakes should be captured as memories if they are clear grammar errors — this helps track whether they become patterns over future conversations.

Tag memories by grammar category:
- word-choice
- verb-agreement
- tense-consistency
- common-confusion
- pronunciation-spelling (when speech-to-text reveals a pattern)
- repetitive-pattern (when the same mistake appears multiple times)

Ignore:
- Style preferences (e.g., starting sentences with "And")
- Filler words ("um", "like", "you know")
- Dialect or colloquial speech
